Overview

BESS Automatic Dimensioning Tool

A Monte-Carlo simulation built on limited historical data, supplemented by Poisson probability distributions, to optimise BESS dimensioning for fast-charging sites under grid connection, power and energy capacity constraints. The resulting configurations are integrated into the company’s business plan to maximise project IRR.

Business impact

As owner of the business plan, I use the tool as a decision-making instrument for the grid connection, the BESS solution and the site configuration. BESS solutions dimensioned with the tool improved project IRR by 2.5 points on average across the projects developed; the results were presented to, and acknowledged by, the private equity infrastructure fund.

Input data

Charging sessions

About one year of charging sessions from a few operating sites. Data cleaning removes outliers such as test sessions and failed starts (under 3 kWh, or outside 3 min–4 h). The resulting dataset is too small to dimension on directly, so additional data is generated in the next step, the demand generator.

Logic and AI

Demand generator

Learns patterns from the real sessions and generates new days with a Poisson law, using day-of-week and month factors. Every date is generated many times, so the rare peak days that drive the dimensioning are covered.

Example data generationPoisson-based synthetic demand

Only about one year of real sessions exists for a few sites, which is far too little to test rare peak days. The tool therefore uses the real data to learn patterns (how many cars, when they arrive, how they charge) and generates new days from them:

λ = sessions per connector per day × connectors × day-of-week factor × month factor
P(k cars) = λk · e−λ / k!  // Poisson law: mean λ, spread √λ

Each date of the year is generated many times (e.g. 30 runs ≈ 11,000 days), so the tool sees quiet days, average days and the rare extreme days that drive BESS dimensioning.

Business context

ChallengeFast-charging sites face multiple constraints: limited grid connection capacity, capacity and commodity tariffs, and available space. An undersized BESS loses sessions and revenue at peak times; an oversized one destroys value and project IRR through capex.
ApproachI built a digital twin of the fast-charging station and generated realistic demand from a limited dataset using Poisson laws. Each BESS option is tested in a Monte-Carlo model simulating every minute of the target year under all constraints, supporting a clear go / no-go decision. The tool then automates the dimensioning: it tests the BESS solutions and returns the most suitable one for the site.
Business impactAs owner of the business plan, I use the tool as a decision-making instrument for the grid connection, the BESS solution and the site configuration. BESS solutions dimensioned with the tool improved project IRR by 2.5 points on average across the projects developed; the results were presented to, and acknowledged by, the private equity infrastructure fund.

Stack: Python · JavaScript · Excel/VBA · AI-assisted development

Input parameters

Service-level ruleA session fails below a set share of requested power (e.g. 85%) for more than a set duration (e.g. 5 min).
Acceptance targetsMaximum share of days with an energy-constrained failure and with a power-constrained failure.
Operating limitsOptional caps on BESS output by weekday and state of charge, to protect the battery or reserve energy.
Search settingsFirst and largest capacity tested, precision, rounding step and safety margin for the automatic search.

Dimensioning algorithm

  1. Phase 1: starting from a small capacity, the tool doubles the BESS size until both acceptance targets are met.
  2. Phase 2: it narrows the interval between the last failing and the first passing size until the required precision is reached.
  3. Final check: the selected size is re-simulated with full accuracy; if a target is missed, a safety margin is added.
  4. Power is searched within the allowed C-rate range, so the result is a complete specification: kWh + kW.

Model architecture

Power flow modelMinute-step dispatch

  1. Queueing: an arriving vehicle takes a free connector or waits.
  2. Power sharing: charger output is split between its connectors.
  3. Grid supply up to the connection limit, then BESS discharge within its power and energy limits; surplus grid capacity recharges the BESS.
  4. Curtailment: if supply is short, all sessions are reduced pro rata.
Pgrid = min(Pdemand, Pconnection)
PBESS = min(Pdemand − Pgrid, Pdischarge,max, Estored / Δt)
Estored(t+1) = Estored(t) − PBESS·Δt + η · Psurplus·Δt

Service-level criterionDefinition of a failed session

Energy-constrained failureThe BESS was empty during the shortfall: more capacity (kWh) is needed.
Power-constrained failureEnergy was available but power was insufficient: more power (kW) is needed.

Failure analysisOne point per simulated day

Each dot is one simulated day. Above the capacity threshold, energy-constrained failures grow with traffic.

Validation and business-plan integration

  • Poisson assumption tested against observed daily session counts.
  • Method back-tested on operating sites against recorded outcomes.
  • Independent Excel/VBA implementation, linked to the business plan, reproduces the results within a few tenths of a percentage point.
  • Results by configuration feed the internal business plan, where the IRR of each grid connection and BESS option is compared.

Overview

Energy Asset Health-Check Agent

A scheduled AI agent that performs a recurring health check of all energy assets, to ensure efficient operation and fast troubleshooting across the site portfolio.

Users and decisions

Asset management receives a structured weekly email highlighting every potential malfunction across the portfolio, each with the recommended remedial action defined during the agent’s implementation.

Input data

Charging session

Every charging session recorded by the network operator, asset by asset.

Relations

  • recorded on → Energy asset
Input data

Grid meter reading

Quarter-hourly energy measured at the grid meter of each site.

Relations

  • measured at → Site
Input data

Meter-to-site mapping

Session data and metering data do not share the same site references. A validated meter-to-site mapping makes the two comparable.

Relations

  • links → Energy asset
  • links → Site
Business object

Energy asset

A charger or charge point. Each one is judged against its own usage pattern, since traffic differs widely from one site to another.

Relations

  • Charging session → recorded on
  • Meter-to-site mapping → links
  • checked by → Detection rules
Business object

Site

A charging station with its grid meter: the level at which metered energy and session energy are reconciled.

Relations

  • Grid meter reading → measured at
  • Meter-to-site mapping → links
  • checked by → Detection rules
Input data

Past incidents

Known incidents, replayed on history to back-test the rules. The energy reconciliation rule reproduced a historical multi-week reporting outage.

Relations

  • back-test → Detection rules
Logic and AI

Detection rules

Four checks: inactivity, failed sessions, underperformance, and reconciliation of the energy recorded in sessions with the energy measured at the grid meter.

Relations

One finding per asset and rule. Issues that have cleared since the last run are removed, so only open problems are raised.

Relations

Metered consumption without recorded sessions points to a data-transmission fault. No consumption and no sessions points to a technical fault.

Business context

ChallengeMonitoring of the charger fleet relied on a legacy central supervision system: staff were expected to act on emails and notifications whenever an asset raised an error. This was highly inefficient. Automation was needed for more frequent and reliable health checks, maximising availability, driver satisfaction and revenue while removing manual work.
ApproachI implemented a scheduled agent that analyses all accessible data sources. With AI, I mapped the structure of every dataset, identified the alarming patterns that can occur, and defined correlation and detection rules across datasets that reveal a possible asset malfunction.
Users and decisionsAsset management receives a structured weekly email highlighting every potential malfunction across the portfolio, each with the recommended remedial action defined during the agent’s implementation.

Detection rules and diagnosis logic

InactivityNo session for longer than the asset's usage pattern makes plausible.
Failed sessionsRepeated starts ending with negligible energy delivered.
UnderperformanceSustained charging well below the power the vehicle and charger allow.
Energy reconciliationEnergy recorded in sessions compared with energy measured at the grid meter.

EV Site Portfolio Management Agent

An AI agent that runs the operational follow-up of a national roll-out of fast-charging sites: it keeps a complete portfolio overview up to date, prepares communications and meetings, and tracks every work package from permit to go-live.

Users and decisions

The agent is fed with incoming information and knows which documents to update, which emails to prepare and to whom, and which meetings to prepare and when. Before a meeting with the architect, for instance, it delivers a brief on every ongoing project with that architect.
